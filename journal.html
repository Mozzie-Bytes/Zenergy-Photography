Perth Convention Centre

Everyone a winner at Hyrox

I went along to Hyrox at the Perth Convention Centre on 22 August to watch someone compete. Thousands of people, each running 8 km broken up by a hard physical station every kilometre, racing the clock and themselves. Some would have set personal bests, a few would have won their heats, and in my book every one of them was a winner.

It was a new kind of shoot for me: indoor light, fast action, nothing I'd tried before. I stayed mostly on the person I'd come to watch but caught a few others along the way, faces grimacing, sweat beading. Four frames are in the In the Zone gallery, the last taken just as the person I'd come to watch triumphantly finished. There'll be more on Instagram.

Stirling Range National Park

Orchids don't wait for you to find your footing

The Stirling Ranges hold some of the best orchid country in the state, and none of it is convenient — most of what's worth photographing is low, small, and half-hidden in leaf litter. Getting the light right meant squatting low or on the ground more often than standing. A few of those frames ended up in the Wildflowers roll, deliberately without exact locations attached — some of what grows out there doesn't need visitors finding it twice.

Learn about the pine from the pine, and the bamboo from the bamboo. after Bashō

Albany

Where the coast stops being polite

Went down to Albany for a sunset. The limestone meets the Southern Ocean like it's still arguing about it. Then the sun broke through and the whole sky caught. Three of those frames are in Coastlines and Golden Hour.

Margaret River

Slow water, slower mornings

Not every roll needs drama. Spent time by a river just watching the light change instead of chasing it — no rush, no single shot to get before the moment passed. Some of the calmest water I've shot all year, which turns out to be its own kind of difficult: nothing to anchor a composition to except what's actually there.

The highest good is like water: it nourishes everything and never competes. after Laozi

Perth Hills

Autumn is when the forest floor finally does something

Fungi season in the Perth Hills is short and weather-dependent — go a week too early and there's nothing, a week too late and it's all gone soft. Spent three mornings on hands and knees along the same stretch of jarrah forest for a handful of frames. The best one took an hour of waiting for the light to drop low enough to backlight the gills without blowing out the cap.

Spring comes, and the grass grows by itself. a Zen saying
