Perth Convention Centre

Everyone a winner at Hyrox

I went along to the recent Hyrox event at the Perth Convention Centre on 22 August to watch someone compete, and it was fantastic. There were thousands of people there, all taking on a gruelling physical test against the clock and against themselves. Each athlete runs 8 km, broken up by a station of hard physical work at every kilometre: SkiErg, sled push, sled pull, burpee broad jumps, rowing, a farmers carry, sandbag lunges and finally wall balls. Everyone loved it and everyone gave their all. Some would have set personal bests, a few would have won their heats, and in my book every one of them was a winner.

It was also a new kind of shoot for me. The light was indoors and the action was fast, and I'd never tried to capture anything like it before. I stayed mostly on the person I'd come to watch, but caught a few others along the way: faces grimacing, sweat beading. I was happy with quite a few of the frames, and four of them are in the In the Zone gallery, the last taken immediately after the person I'd come to watch triumphantly finished. There'll be more on Instagram.

Stirling Range National Park

Orchids don't wait for you to find your footing

The Stirling Ranges hold some of the best orchid country in the state, and none of it is convenient — most of what's worth photographing is low, small, and half-hidden in leaf litter. Getting the light right meant squating low or on the ground more often than standing. A few of those frames ended up in the Wildflowers roll, deliberately without exact locations attached — some of what grows out there doesn't need visitors finding it twice.

Albany

Where the coast stops being polite

The limestone down around Albany doesn't do gentle — the rock meets the Southern Ocean like it's still arguing about it. Went out chasing a sunset. Worth it! When the Sunlight broke through the whole sky lit up. With the Great Southern Ocean as a backdrop it was truly stunning

Margaret River

Slow water, slower mornings

Not every roll needs drama. Spent time by a river just watching the light change instead of chasing it — no rush, no single shot to get before the moment passed. Some of the calmest water I've shot all year, which turns out to be its own kind of difficult: nothing to anchor a composition to except what's actually there.

Perth Hills

Autumn is when the forest floor finally does something

Fungi season in the Perth Hills is short and weather-dependent — go a week too early and there's nothing, a week too late and it's all gone soft. Spent three mornings on hands and knees along the same stretch of jarrah forest for a handful of frames. The best one took an hour of waiting for the light to drop low enough to backlight the gills without blowing out the cap.
