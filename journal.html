Notes from the field

Short write-ups from wherever the last roll was shot, or whenever I get around to it. Some are written with a little help from AI.

Scarborough Skate Park

Upside-down more than right-side-up

The BMX Bowl Battle returned to Scarborough Skate Park on Saturday 10 October, part of Groundswell, at one of Australia's most recognised skate bowls. It was a knockout format, with the Little RADs first and then the RADs and the RADDEST. I've covered the Little RADs on Instagram, so the frames here are from the two senior divisions. The riders were fast. They jumped high. They spun fast. Some seemed to be upside-down more than right-side-up.

The day started with a bit of luck. I got on the right side of the fence straight away, which put me right up close, and I was determined to make the most of it. The weather looked like it would spoil the show, but after a short rain interlude it finished with the Red Bull Highest Air. Samual Grace easily won it, probably not surprising to anyone, and I'm fairly sure he touched the sun.

I worried I'd misjudged my shutter speed in the changing light and underestimated how fast the riders were, but a good number of frames seem “ok” to me. The point is to capture the moment, and I did that 2,684 times on Saturday. Every one of you gave it 100% all day, and then some. I don't know every rider or their name, but I sure enjoyed capturing the moment with you all.

One rider I want to point out is Jay Furukawa. He doesn't turn up much on my Instagram, but I really like the frame of him carving the lip under that stormy sky. The one where he flies through the handlebars of a bike in the foreground deserves credit too. He took third in the RADDEST.

The RAD: 1 Jaia Forte, 2 Joel Weller, 3 Tate Wanless, 4 Blake Wylie, 5 Garnet Rock, 6 Serg Potekhin, 7 Pacey Park.
The RADDEST: 1 Samual Grace, 2 Jacob Roughton, 3 Jay Furukawa, 4 Charlie Hill, 5 Liam Rhodes, 6 Billy Gibson.

Some of the boys shredding included @samual.grace, @garnetrock_bmx, @charliehill.bmx, @tate.wanless, @liamrhodes.bmx, @roughton_jacob, @jaia.rides, @billy.gibsn, @theo_bootwash_bmx, @theziggy_show and @jay.furukawa, with @bamm_b_djofficial keeping the soundtrack going on the decks.

I was glad to see one of the sponsors, Right By You, backing mental health, which is a foundation of this page. A lot of these riders could be called misfits, but in the bowl they fit, and they have a family there. Thanks to Freestyle Now, Red Bull, City of Stirling, Colony BMX and Odyssey BMX, and to Sneaky Wholefoods for supporting the day. Groundswell is delivered by the City of Stirling, the Scarborough Beach Association and Freestyle Now.

The frames are in In the Zone, with a few close-ups, and the DJ, in Portraits. The storm that rolled past between sessions is in Thunderbolts & Lightning.

Fremantle, Grand Final Day

Wharfie Time, interrupted

The day was filled with excitement and nervous anticipation. This was a team that looked certain to win its first Grand Final. The crowds packed every train to Freo, the pubs were overflowing with very, very long queues, and the streets were teeming with eager spectators. We watched the game at the Fremantle Navy Club bar on High Street, filled to capacity. (Be sure you support them at their new location soon)

The game was an epic, but it was not to be for Freo. With about four minutes to go they led by 17 points and Wharfie Time had been sounded. Then Brisbane kicked the last four goals, the final one with twelve seconds left, to win by seven points. The room fell silent. Tears and groans were seen and heard. Small consolation: Shai Bolton took the Norm Smith Medal, only the fifth player to win it from the losing side.

The morning crowd is in the Visual Storytelling and Streets galleries: the surge through Fremantle Station, and then the street itself, scarves and bunting everywhere. The mirrored street signs on High and Pakenham Streets are in Streets too. The faces in the bar, including the moment the room erupted after an early goal, are in Portraits, and I'm grateful to the guest who were all keen for me to share them. I hope they turned out "OK", (many did not!). I decided not to take photos at the end of the game. I do wish I captured that moment of raw human disappointment.

After the game we drove down to King Road Brewing Co in Oldbury. Great food and a great venue, and a good place to let the day settle. The windmill is in Life, the Universe & Everything and an old Trader truck is in Worn, Not Broken.

I have no doubt it will be Wharfie Time again before too long, and a Grand Final victory to follow.

Zig Zag, Perth Hills

The city stood still. The cars didn't.

Spent the day at the Zig Zag stage of Tarmac West. The view across Perth is spectacular, the city a thin skyline on the horizon, and the sky was heavy and overcast with rain on the way. Lovely clouds, but the action was on the road.

I used a slow shutter and panned with the cars, giving up most of each frame to blur on purpose. The hillside streaks, the road streaks, and now and then the driver comes out sharp. My favourite is the one where the skyline stays perfectly still while the car dissolves in front of it. #2, Daniel and Colin McGuckin, took the T130 class by 23 seconds. More frames are in the In the Zone gallery, and the wildflowers I shot earlier that morning, before the cars arrived, are in Wildflowers.

Perth Convention Centre

Everyone a winner at Hyrox

I went along to Hyrox at the Perth Convention Centre on 22 August to watch someone compete. Thousands of people, each running 8 km broken up by a hard physical station every kilometre, racing the clock and themselves. Some would have set personal bests, a few would have won their heats, and in my book every one of them was a winner.

It was a new kind of shoot for me: indoor light, fast action, nothing I'd tried before. I stayed mostly on the person I'd come to watch but caught a few others along the way, faces grimacing, sweat beading. Four frames are in the In the Zone gallery, the last taken just as the person I'd come to watch triumphantly finished. There'll be more on Instagram.

Stirling Range National Park

Orchids don't wait for you to find your footing

The Stirling Ranges hold some of the best orchid country in the state, and none of it is convenient — most of what's worth photographing is low, small, and half-hidden in leaf litter. Getting the light right meant squatting low or on the ground more often than standing. A few of those frames ended up in the Wildflowers roll, deliberately without exact locations attached — some of what grows out there doesn't need visitors finding it twice.

Learn about the pine from the pine, and the bamboo from the bamboo. after Bashō

Albany

Where the coast stops being polite

Went down to Albany for a sunset. The limestone meets the Southern Ocean like it's still arguing about it. Then the sun broke through and the whole sky caught. Three of those frames are in Coastlines and Golden Hour.

Margaret River

Slow water, slower mornings

Not every roll needs drama. Spent time by a river just watching the light change instead of chasing it — no rush, no single shot to get before the moment passed. Some of the calmest water I've shot all year, which turns out to be its own kind of difficult: nothing to anchor a composition to except what's actually there.

The highest good is like water: it nourishes everything and never competes. after Laozi

Perth Hills

Autumn is when the forest floor finally does something

Fungi season in the Perth Hills is short and weather-dependent — go a week too early and there's nothing, a week too late and it's all gone soft. Spent three mornings on hands and knees along the same stretch of jarrah forest for a handful of frames. The best one took an hour of waiting for the light to drop low enough to backlight the gills without blowing out the cap.

Spring comes, and the grass grows by itself. a Zen saying
