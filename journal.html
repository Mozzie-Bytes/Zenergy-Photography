Stirling Range National Park

Orchids don't wait for you to find your footing

The Stirling Ranges hold some of the best orchid country in the state, and none of it is convenient — most of what's worth photographing is low, small, and half-hidden in leaf litter. Getting the light right meant lying flat on the ground more often than standing. A few of those frames ended up in the Wildflowers roll, deliberately without exact locations attached — some of what grows out there doesn't need visitors finding it twice.

Albany

Where the coast stops being polite

The limestone down around Albany doesn't do gentle — the rock meets the Southern Ocean like it's still arguing about it. Went out chasing a storm cell rolling in off the water and got maybe four usable frames out of an hour of getting sandblasted. Worth it for the one where the light broke through right as the swell hit the rocks.

Margaret River

Slow water, slower mornings

Not every roll needs drama. Spent a morning by the river just watching the light change instead of chasing it — no rush, no single shot to get before the moment passed. Some of the calmest water I've shot all year, which turns out to be its own kind of difficult: nothing to anchor a composition to except what's actually there.

Perth Hills

Autumn is when the forest floor finally does something

Fungi season in the Perth Hills is short and weather-dependent — go a week too early and there's nothing, a week too late and it's all gone soft. Spent three mornings on hands and knees along the same stretch of jarrah forest for a handful of frames. The best one took an hour of waiting for the light to drop low enough to backlight the gills without blowing out the cap.
